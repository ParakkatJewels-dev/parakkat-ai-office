**AI Office: quality review, performance marketing and business follow-through**

Review proposal · 26 October 2026

The recommendation is to extend the existing 67 nonhuman staff roles with three specialists, creating a proposed roster of 70: 67 model-agent roles and three existing sync bots. Roles are responsibilities, not 70 continuously running model sessions. Existing human heads remain accountable for their departments.

The user has agreed with the proposed three-agent direction. This document sets out the detailed scope for review; the additions and workflows below have not been implemented. The review used the local repository, not a fresh inspection of live advertising accounts. It does not establish that any particular current campaign is failing.

**What needs to change**

AI Office already has performance, analytics, strategy, creative, QA, inventory, customer-service and leadership roles. It also has advertising alerts, replacement-copy proposals, head-task delegation and human approvals. The missing connection is reliable follow-through from a proposal to confirmed execution, measured results, correction and a justified next step.

There are also quality defects to fix first: some older review paths can forward work despite an unresolved negative review. Existing learning rules can infer profit or recommend budget movement from simple ROAS rankings. Adding more agents without correcting these paths would multiply unreliable work.

**Three new agents**

| Proposed agent | Team and accountable head | Responsibilities and required output |
|---|---|---|
| Output QC Reviewer | QA; existing Compliance Checker becomes QA/QC Lead | Independently inspect completed work against the brief, evidence and acceptance criteria. Record pass, needs correction, or blocked, with precise reasons and the checked version. Never approve its own work. |
| Funnel/CRO Specialist | Performance; Performance Lead, with E-com Manager involved for store changes | Locate supported conversion problems from landing page through purchase; distinguish tracking gaps from customer drop-off. Produce a diagnosis, evidence, proposed fix, implementation owner and measurement plan. |
| Experiment & Outcomes Analyst | Insights; Analytics Lead, working with the Performance Lead | Maintain baselines, hypotheses, experiment history and evaluation dates. Evaluate whether a change helped, harmed or remains inconclusive. Produce evidence for the head's next decision, including scaling readiness. |

These additions do not replace current specialists. Measurement QA belongs to Attribution Analyst and Data Hub Lead. Ads Analyst owns campaign surveillance. Performance Lead and Media Planner own scaling proposals, supported by Budget Guard and Forecaster. A software-based delivery tracker handles deadlines and reminders; it does not need another model agent.

**QA/QC team and approval rules**

QA prevents avoidable defects before work starts: the QA/QC Lead defines required inputs, acceptance criteria and relevant checks. QC examines the completed output: the new reviewer checks the actual deliverable, with specialist checks selected only when relevant.

| Reviewer | Checks |
|---|---|
| Output QC Reviewer | Task requirements, source support, calculations, completeness and actual deliverable existence |
| Price Auditor | Product identity, price, discount arithmetic, dates and stock claims |
| Policy Checker / QA/QC Lead | Supported jewellery claims, publication requirements and unresolved risk flags |
| Brand Guardian / Voice Keeper / language specialists | Product fidelity, visual and verbal consistency, legibility and language quality |
| Attribution Analyst / Data Hub Lead | Measurement definitions, freshness, coverage, missing data and reconciliation |
| Budget Guard | Approved spending envelope, numerical limits and commercial guardrails |

Deterministic checks run first. One suitable independent model reviewer handles semantic quality when needed; the whole team does not review every output. A creator cannot supply the independent verdict. An AI policy review is advisory and does not guarantee platform acceptance.

The normal route is: **brief → head plan → specialist work → head review → independent QC → human decision**. The head remains accountable throughout. A failed mandatory check blocks approval and execution. Exhausting the revision allowance creates a blocker; it must never convert a failure into approval.

Bind review and human approval to the exact text, image/file, prices, audience, budget and evidence version being approved. Relevant changes invalidate the affected approval. A generated image must exist and have passed its required artifact checks; a description of an image is not a finished image.

Allow one targeted correction round shared by head review and QC. Preserve passed work, rerun only affected checks and stop for human direction if the blocker remains. QA receives access only to the assigned artifact and permitted supporting evidence through server-controlled routing, without broad access to unrelated departments or private customer records.

**Monitoring existing campaigns as well as new ones**

Create a campaign registry from the selected, accessible Meta and Google accounts. Include campaigns created outside AI Office and campaigns with no spend or impressions. Record account and provider IDs, hierarchy, current delivery status, budget, objective, destination, assets, owner and last successful refresh. Names are display labels, not unique identifiers.

Show coverage explicitly: connected accounts, unsupported campaign types or fields, permission failures and stale imports. A missing campaign or empty result must not appear as proof that nothing is running. Externally managed campaigns start in observation mode with an assigned monitoring owner; proposed changes require the same human approval as AI-created campaigns.

Track three separate status dimensions:

| Dimension | Example states |
|---|---|
| Work and approval | Proposed; producing; needs correction; QC passed; awaiting human; approved; rejected; expired |
| Implementation | Not started; scheduled; attempted; provider confirmed; uncertain; failed; cancelled |
| Business outcome | Awaiting data; healthy; underperforming; improving; inconclusive; ready for scaling review; closed |

An accepted draft is not an executed change. A returned campaign ID does not establish that ads are delivering. A finished task can still have a pending business-outcome review.

Every initiative receives an owner, linked tasks, acceptance criteria, dependencies, deadline, approved version, implementation evidence, baseline, primary metric, guardrails, evaluation window and next review date. Approval has an expiry when its inputs are time-sensitive. Overdue approvals, unimplemented decisions and missed evaluations appear in one shared queue; escalation is shown in the app rather than generating repeated proposals or sending unapproved external messages.

**When a campaign underperforms**

1. Validate data freshness, conversion definitions and reporting coverage. If measurement is incomplete, open a measurement incident and show “insufficient evidence.”
2. Check actual delivery: rejection, eligibility, exhausted budget, scheduling and destination availability.
3. Investigate audience and traffic, creative, landing/product experience, checkout, price, stock and fulfilment constraints. Choose the investigation supported by the evidence.
4. Propose a specific hypothesis and change, with expected effect, primary metric, guardrails, test cost, evaluation date and implementation owner.
5. Obtain QC and human approval for the exact action; then verify implementation through a provider receipt or verifiable human completion evidence.
6. Evaluate after the agreed observation window. Record improvement, no improvement, harmful change or inconclusive evidence. Create a next action with an owner rather than silently closing the task.

Do not label a recent campaign a failure before accounting for conversion delay. Google advises evaluating automated bidding changes over conversion cycles so late conversions can be included; the plan will set the relevant window per campaign rather than use one universal daily cutoff. [Google Ads: how bidding algorithms learn](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10970825?hl=en).

One approved investigation can propose a bounded alternative test. A second test needs its own approval or an explicitly preapproved test envelope. Two unsuccessful tests against the same hypothesis trigger a strategy review instead of endless creative regeneration. Serious deterioration creates an urgent recommendation; automatic pause or rollback is available only under an explicitly approved action policy.

Illustrative flow, not a finding about your accounts: a campaign brings product visits but few completed purchases → validate checkout-event coverage → investigate the affected device/product segment → propose a supported checkout or offer fix → approve and verify the change → evaluate the result → either propose scaling or return a clear diagnosis and next hypothesis.

**When a change succeeds**

The Experiment & Outcomes Analyst evaluates whether the apparent win has sufficient evidence. Where feasible, use a comparison/control with a prespecified metric and evaluation window. A simple before/after change remains observational; seasonality, offers and other simultaneous changes may explain it.

Performance Lead and Media Planner then prepare a scaling proposal only if tracking is healthy, results have matured, economics are acceptable and stock, fulfilment and cash capacity can support expansion. Budget Guard checks the approved ceiling. Forecaster checks product capacity. Scaling has a defined increment, maximum spend, observation period, stop conditions and reversion plan. There is no universal “increase every budget by 20%” rule.

Platform-attributed ROAS is not profit. Where data permits, use net sales and agreed product, payment, fulfilment and return costs to evaluate contribution after advertising. Keep platform-attributed conversions separate from reconciled orders; do not add Meta and Google attributed purchases together as unique sales. Without trustworthy costs, report performance and missing economics rather than declare a profitable winner.

After an approved scale change, evaluate its incremental effect, not just the historical average. A winner can become inefficient at a larger budget. Record the outcome and decide whether to hold, expand, revise or propose reversal.

**Funnel diagnosis**

Observe: **ad click → landing visit → product view → add to cart → checkout → purchase → fulfilment/return → repeat purchase**.

GA4 ecommerce supports product-view, cart, checkout, purchase and refund events. These events must be implemented and verified before the specialist can locate corresponding losses. [Google Analytics ecommerce measurement](https://developers.google.com/analytics/devguides/collection/ga4/ecommerce).

Use compatible cohorts and explicit denominators, segmented by channel, device, landing page and product where volume permits. Keep acquisition-to-site comparisons separate when cross-system identity or consent coverage prevents a reliable joined funnel. Record event coverage and transaction deduplication. Shipping, payment failure and checkout-error diagnosis require their own events or operational data; abandonment alone does not identify the cause.

The current connector has only a GA4 channel summary function, with no caller found in the repository audit, and no implemented step-level funnel. Google Ads also currently maps generic conversions into a purchases field. Both need correction before purchase-specific comparisons or full funnel diagnosis can be trusted.

**What you would see in AI Office**

- An Outcomes board showing every initiative's owner, approval state, execution state, result, next action and review date.
- A Campaign Health view including current external campaigns, delivery/pacing, data freshness, evidence and open incidents.
- A QA Inbox with exact checked versions, failures, requested corrections and human decisions.
- A Funnel view showing measured stages, affected segments and explicit tracking gaps.
- An Experiments view showing hypotheses, baselines, changes, evaluation dates and scaling decisions.
- Agent profiles showing responsibilities, current instructions, assignments, dependencies and evidence-backed status.
- Office-map dialogue tied to real handoffs and findings. Waiting for data or human approval appears as waiting; it does not create fake activity, meetings or model calls.

Each recommendation card explains what changed, why it matters, the evidence, proposed action, expected benefit, possible downside, owner and review date. Human review offers approve, request revision, reject or defer. Approve authorizes only the stated action and limits.

**Token and repetition controls**

Routine monitoring uses scheduled data reads, SQL/calculations, thresholds and deadline rules. Unchanged evidence triggers zero model analysis calls. All agents share the same compact evidence snapshot instead of repeatedly loading all files or querying the same account.

Use stable fingerprints, one open incident per entity/problem/window, cooldowns, cached completed stages, a persistent call ledger and a global work queue. Timeouts with uncertain model or provider outcomes require reconciliation; they must not cause blind repeats. Reaching a budget limit queues work and shows the reason.

Start with one consolidated daily performance review and exceptions triggered by material new evidence. A weekly strategy review only uses new results. Data-refresh cadence is configured per source; an hourly software check cannot make a daily provider metric real time. The daily digest is produced inside the app, with external delivery enabled only if separately requested.

Before activating the pilot, enforce budget admission for every scheduled analysis and review provider, including text and vision calls. The current central guard covers `chatJSON`, while direct Gemini text/vision and configured Jev calls can bypass it. A whole-office allowance is only meaningful after these paths share admission and usage accounting. Image generation needs its own explicit count/cost limits; a text-token ceiling does not limit image spend.

Preserve the current configured application ceilings and manual pause during implementation. For the pilot, reserve at least half of daily and weekly call/token allowance for human-assigned work and cap scheduled analysis at the remaining half, whichever limit is reached first. With the currently recorded limits, that scheduled allocation is at most 20 calls/100,000 tokens per day and 100 calls/500,000 tokens per week across the whole office, not per agent. These are ceilings, not usage targets, and model-account quotas can still be reached independently.

Route simple extraction, arithmetic and checks through code. Use low-reasoning model calls for concise interpretation. Send only evidence relevant to the assigned task. Do not run all staff daily or let agent conversation itself create additional tasks. A head-task round with independent QC should have a fixed shared call allowance, initially at most eight calls including one targeted correction; stop earlier whenever it is complete or blocked. Any change to current per-task limits must be explicitly visible in the implementation plan and remain inside global budgets.

**Implementation order and acceptance criteria**

| Phase | Deliverables | Required proof before the next phase |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Quality and reliable records | Fix negative-review handoffs; introduce independent QC and version-bound approval; add initiative/outcome records; correct metric identity and conversion definitions; enforce provider-wide budget admission | Failed checks never pass; changed versions invalidate approval; scoped access holds; no double handoff or repeat calls on retries; no scheduled analysis/review route bypasses its budget; images have separate limits |
| 2 — Read-only monitoring | Import selected account campaign inventory; establish baselines; campaign health and outcome board; funnel coverage assessment; department follow-through | Existing external campaigns appear; zero-activity entities are represented; partial/stale data is visible; missing events are not called abandonment; unchanged monitoring uses no model calls |
| 3 — Diagnosis and experiments | Add the two analytical specialists, bounded investigations, reviewable changes and experiment evaluation | Tracking faults, delivery faults and underperformance are distinguished; every proposed action has an owner and deadline; no causal win is invented from weak evidence |
| 4 — Approved implementation and scaling | Add provider-specific actions as needed, exact approved payloads, receipts, reconciliation and monitored scaling | External writes cannot duplicate; changed live settings require re-review; uncertain outcomes are reconciled; caps and reversal conditions work; Google/Meta capabilities are tested separately |
| 5 — Department expansion | Apply the shared loop to all listed sections; consider optional operational teams when their data exists | Each workflow has an observable business result or an explicit limitation, and demonstrated reduction of human follow-up within usage budgets |

The initial launch should cover phases 1 and 2 first, followed by a read-only pilot on selected accounts. Pilot duration must include enough provider refreshes and relevant conversion delay to evaluate the checks; it should not be declared successful solely because a few days elapsed. Later phases require evidence from that pilot and review of their concrete action scope.

Test scenarios include an unsupported price claim, an absent image, a third negative review, two same-name campaigns in different accounts, a non-purchase Google conversion, stale/partial imports, a missing checkout event, a delayed purchase, an expired approval, an external campaign edited after approval, a write timeout, repeated scheduler ticks, and token-budget exhaustion. Verify lifecycle transitions and visible outcomes using fixtures/mocks first; paid or external-action tests need their own explicit scope.

Before activating recommendations, configure the accounts in scope, campaign objectives, human owners, acceptable acquisition cost, commercial definitions, test budget, minimum evidence and review windows. Unknown thresholds stay unset and block dependent decisions; the system must not invent them.
